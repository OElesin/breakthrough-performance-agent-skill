# Fact 10: Innovation Fulcrum ("Model T") Analysis
**Law:** Law 4 — Simplicity gets results
**Position in Law:** Fact 1 of 3

---

## Purpose
Find the point where your products or services meet customer needs with the lowest possible level of complexity — your innovation fulcrum — and quantify exactly what unnecessary complexity is costing you in margin, revenue, and organisational capacity.

---

## Goal
The objective of zero-base complexity analysis is to find your innovation fulcrum: the point where products or services meet customer needs with the lowest possible level of complexity.

This is a significant challenge because conventional management accounting systems focus only on the incremental costs of complexity. The Model T approach addresses the **total systems cost of complexity** by beginning with the cost of one single representative product — as if that were the company's only offering — then adding variation back in step by step, tracking both the cost effects and the true revenue impact of each addition.

As noted in Chapter 5 of The Breakthrough Imperative: eliminating unnecessary complexity or postponing complexity until later in the value chain have been shown to increase profits.

A Model T analysis answers questions such as:
- How many products or services should we have?
- Which SKUs might we eliminate or manage differently?
- How much is complexity costing us?
- Do we understand the root causes of our complexity?
- Do we have the capabilities to manage complexity effectively?

---

## Diagnostic Question
> "How complex are your products or service offerings, and what is that degree of complexity costing you? Where is your innovation fulcrum? What are the Killer ABCs in your business?"

---

## Data Required

- **Estimates of costs and revenues at zero complexity**
  - What it is: What would it cost and what revenue would you generate if you made one single "average" product or service — your Model T equivalent
  - Where to get it: Finance, operations, product management

- **Estimates of how costs and revenues change as complexity is layered back in**
  - What it is: Step-by-step impact on costs, revenues, processes, and quality as each product variant or service option is added
  - Where to get it: Finance, sales data, customer research, operational analysis

---

## Analytical Approach

### Two complementary charts (Figure A3.23)

**Chart 1 — Cost Per Unit vs. Number of Customer Choices:**
Shows where step changes in cost occur as product variety increases, and what it would cost to make one "average" product or service. Reveals the true systems cost of variety — not just the incremental cost of adding one more SKU.

**Chart 2 — Total Revenue vs. Number of Customer Choices:**
Shows the revenue curve as product variety increases — rising initially, peaking (where are revenues maximised?), then declining as salesforce confusion or customers giving up causes revenue to fall despite more choice being offered.

The intersection of these two charts reveals the innovation fulcrum — the complexity level that maximises the profit spread between revenue and cost.

### Understanding the Killer ABCs
The Killer ABCs are the key attributes, benefits, and costs that determine which complexity elements should be added back. They represent what customers in each segment most value. Any SKU or service variant that does not meet the Killer ABCs for key segments should not be added back.

### Four-step process (Figure A3.24)

| Step | Description | Key Success Factor |
|------|-------------|---------------------|
| 1. Calculate revenues and costs of your Model T equivalent | Establish the zero-complexity baseline | Estimate costs and revenues for the "one product" or service process and assess the impact on quality |
| 2. Understand customer needs | Determine which complexity adds real value | Rigorously analyse the Killer ABCs to decide which complexity elements should be added back |
| 3. Quantify effect of adding complexity back in | Build up complexity step by step | Step-by-step is critical: what would revenues and costs be with two product offerings? three? four? Quantify how processes, revenues, costs, and quality change as complexity is layered back in |
| 4. Find your innovation fulcrum and draw implications | Identify the optimal complexity level | Managing complexity does NOT mean blindly reducing SKUs — many may appear unprofitable due to high fixed-cost allocation; use product-line profitability analysis (Fact 3) to determine true profits. Ensure SKUs added back meet Killer ABCs for key segments |

## Interpretation Guide

**Healthy signal:**
- Revenue curve peaks well before your current complexity level — you have clear room to simplify and grow margin
- Killer ABCs are clearly defined and product portfolio tightly aligns to them

**Warning signal:**
- Costs are rising steeply with each complexity addition while revenue curve has already plateaued — you are past your innovation fulcrum and destroying margin
- Salesforce confusion or customer overwhelm is suppressing revenue despite a wide product range

**Ambiguous result — probe further:**
- If it's unclear where costs step-change, ask: which product variants require unique components, tooling, or processes? Those are the structural cost drivers.
- If the Killer ABCs are unclear, ask: what are the one or two things that reliably tip a customer from considering you to buying?

## Agent Prompting Guidance

**Opening question:**
> "We're into Law 4 now — this is about your organisation's ability to execute. We start with complexity. If you stripped your offering back to a single 'Model T' product or service — one thing that met the core customer need at the lowest possible cost — what would that be? And how far is your current portfolio from that baseline?"

**Follow-up probes if stuck:**
- "Which of your SKUs or service variants generate the most revenue? Which generate the most complexity?"
- "Have you ever seen a customer choose a simpler competitor over you because your range was overwhelming or confusing?"
- "What's the one product variant or feature that your best customers consistently say they can't live without?"
- "If you had to cut your product range by 30% tomorrow, where would you start?"

**Synthesising the finding:**
> "It sounds like your innovation fulcrum is roughly at [complexity level], meaning [current number] of your variants may be past the point of adding revenue worth their cost. The most clearly dispensable complexity looks like [area]. Does that feel right?"

**Signal to move on:**
User has a working sense of where their complexity fulcrum is, has identified which complexity adds genuine customer value versus which is pure overhead, and understands the rough cost of being past their optimum.
