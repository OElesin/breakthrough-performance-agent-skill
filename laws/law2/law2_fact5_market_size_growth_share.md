# Fact 5: Market Size, Growth, and Share Analysis
**Law:** Law 2 — Competitive position determines your options
**Position in Law:** Fact 2 of 3

---

## Purpose
Establish a clear picture of how big your market really is, which parts of it are growing fastest, and where you are gaining or losing ground — the foundation for deciding where to commit resources.

---

## Goal
A market map is a good starting point for diagnosing market growth rates and your competitive position in the market. It shows:
1. The total size of the market
2. Key market segments and the size of each one
3. Market share of competitors in each segment
4. Areas of growth
5. Areas where you are underperforming or overperforming

---

## Diagnostic Question
> "How big is your market? Which parts are growing fastest? Where are you gaining/losing share?"

---

## Data Required

- **Total market size last year and three to five years prior**
  - What it is: Overall market size at two points in time, enabling a growth-rate calculation
  - Where to get it: Industry reports, market research firms, trade associations

- **Sales by competitor last year and three to five years prior, by product or submarket**
  - What it is: Competitor-level sales broken down by segment, at both time points
  - Where to get it: Public filings, analyst reports, industry data providers, triangulated estimates

---

## Analytical Approach

### Four-step market mapping process (Figure A3.10)

| Step | Description | Key Success Factor |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1. Establish correct business definition | Define which competitors belong in your market | Ensure all the right competitors are included; validate by testing whether each has the same types of costs, customers, and capabilities as you |
| 2. Gather top-down estimates for size of total market and each segment | Size the whole market and its sub-segments | Make sure to map even segments where you are not currently participating |
| 3. Gather estimates for sales of top competitors by segment | Allocate competitor revenue across segments | Take out any sales from competitors' totals that are not actually in that segment |
| 4. Compare to market map from 3–5 years ago; draw implications | Identify what has shifted | Consider strategic and operational implications — e.g. which segments to focus resources on, tactics versus specific competitors to gain or protect share |

### Reading the market map (Figure A3.9)
The market map is a mosaic-style chart built for two time points — "3–5 years ago" and "this year":
- **X-axis:** Market segments or products, with column width representing each segment's size
- **Y-axis (within each column):** Competitors stacked by their share of that segment
- **Your company** is shaded distinctly from competitors

Comparing the two charts side by side reveals: which segments have grown or shrunk in absolute size; which competitors have gained or lost share within each segment; and where you personally are over- or under-performing relative to the segment's growth.

---

## Interpretation Guide

**Healthy signal:**
- You are gaining share in the fastest-growing segments — your growth is compounding with the market's growth
- Segments where you are strong are also segments that are expanding

**Warning signal:**
- You are gaining share only in shrinking or stagnant segments while losing share in the fastest-growing ones
- A competitor is capturing disproportionate share of new market growth, suggesting they have better positioning or capabilities for where the market is heading

**Ambiguous result — probe further:**
- If share shifts are unclear, ask: is the change driven by price, volume, or a new entrant?
- If a segment's growth is hard to verify, cross-check using multiple independent sources before drawing conclusions

## Agent Prompting Guidance

**Opening question:**
> "Now let's map your market itself — not just your position within it, but its size, growth, and how share has shifted over the last several years. Do you have a sense of how big your total addressable market is, and which segments within it are growing fastest?"

**Follow-up probes if stuck:**
- "What are the natural segments or sub-markets within your industry?"
- "Looking back 3–5 years, has the overall market grown, shrunk, or stayed flat?"
- "Are there segments you're not currently competing in that are growing quickly?"
- "Where do you suspect you've gained share, and where do you suspect you've lost it?"

**Synthesising the finding:**
> "So the market overall has [grown/shrunk] over the last several years, with [segment] as the fastest grower. You appear to be [gaining/losing] share, particularly in [segment]. Does that match how you'd describe your trajectory?"

**Signal to move on:**
User has a working view of overall market size and growth, has identified which segments are expanding or contracting, and has a rough read on where they are gaining or losing share.
