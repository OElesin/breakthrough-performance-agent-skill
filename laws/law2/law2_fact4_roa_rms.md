# Fact 4: Return on Assets / Relative Market Share
**Law:** Law 2 — Competitive position determines your options
**Position in Law:** Fact 1 of 3

---

## Purpose
Plot your company and all competitors on a single chart that reveals who is winning, why, and by how much — and what your full potential position could be if you close the gap.

---

## Goal
The ROA/RMS chart diagnoses operational underperformance and assesses strategic opportunities and challenges. It can also:
- Provide an estimate of potential synergies available from a merger
- Highlight inaccurate or changing business definitions in ways that can help drive winning strategies
- Track ROA/RMS performance over a complete business cycle (requires at least five years of data)

The logic underlying this chart: companies with greater relative market share have the potential to earn higher profits because of their greater accumulated experience, the lower cost position it permits, and the ability to reinvest more in R&D and customer enhancements. Research has shown that **relative market share is a more powerful predictor of business performance than absolute market share**, and **return on assets is a better measure than return on sales** since it accounts for variation in asset intensity across industries.

---

## Diagnostic Question
> "Where do you and your competitors fall on the ROA/RMS chart? How are the leaders making money, and what is their approach? What is the full potential of your business position?"

---

## Data Required

- **Your company's operating profit and total assets** — this year and last year, to determine average returns earned
- **Same data for all competitors** — operating profit and total assets
- **All competitors' sales this year** — to calculate relative market share

### RMS Formulae
```
Market leader's RMS = (market leader's revenue or units) / (number 2 player's revenue or units)

Any other player's RMS = (that player's revenue or units) / (market leader's revenue or units)
```

**Note on ROA:** Ideally use operating assets = fixed assets + net working capital − excess cash/marketable securities

---

## Analytical Approach

### Five-step ROA/RMS process (Figure A3.8)

| Step | Description | Key Success Factor |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1. Establish correct business definition | Define which competitors belong in your market | Ensure all right competitors are included; validate by testing whether they have same types of costs, customers, and capabilities as you |
| 2. Measure return on assets | Calculate ROA for your company and all competitors | Use operating assets (fixed assets + net working capital − excess cash/marketable securities) |
| 3. Calculate relative market share | RMS for your company and all competitors | Adjust for differences in business portfolio — exclude competitor divisions not part of your business definition |
| 4. Plot normative band | Place all companies on the ROA vs RMS chart | Use regression and judgement to plot the normative band that best fits the data |
| 5. Draw implications | Interpret your position and full potential | Use the ROA/RMS chart to draw strategic implications and insights (refer to Table 3.2 and Chapter 3 for detailed list) |

### Reading the chart (Figure A3.7)
- **X-axis:** Relative Market Share (RMS) on a log scale (0.1 → 10)
- **Y-axis:** Return on Assets (ROA) as a percentage
- **Bubble size:** Sales volume
- **Normative band:** The diagonal band showing the expected ROA for a given RMS level
- **Your position:** Where your bubble sits relative to the band — above means outperforming, below means underperforming for your market position
- **Potential arrow:** Shows the trajectory to improve RMS and move ROA toward the band (e.g. current ROA 4% → potential ROA ~12%)

### Five market positions and their strategic implications (Table 3.2)

| Position | Typical Characteristics | Strategic Imperative | Example Actions |
|----------|------------------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| 1. In-band leaders | High market share, high stable returns; generates cash to outinvest competitors | Stay the course, aggressively | Reinvest to gain share, cut costs, widen profit gap; raise industry standards of excellence; exploit close-in adjacencies |
| 2. Overperformers | Under a price umbrella (danger of collapsing) with disproportionate returns; OR unique innovations/capabilities | Invest to maintain position | Reduce costs to prepare for umbrella collapse; market the innovation effectively; set prices to maximise earnings with aggressive cost management; invest in brand and premium products |
| 3. In-band followers | Solid #2 or #3 player, rarely cost leader, acceptable but fluctuating profitability | Innovate and improve | Invest aggressively: innovate, cut costs, improve loyalty; acquire underperformer for scale; monitor leader for missteps |
| 4. Below-band leaders | Coasting on past performance; not managing costs well; suffering quality problems or lack of innovation; or investing to gain share | Diagnose the reason, invest for leadership | Diagnose underperformance; improve RCP; reduce price leakage; increase customer focus |
| 5. Distant/below-band followers | Low market share and below-average earnings from multiple causes | Up or out | Merge with larger competitor; differentiate for niche segments; pursue very aggressive improvement actions; use the band for M&A portfolio decisions |

## Interpretation Guide

**Healthy signal:**
- Your bubble sits on or above the normative band — you are earning the return your market position warrants or better
- RMS is ≥ 1.0 — you are the market leader in your defined business

**Warning signal:**
- Your bubble sits significantly below the band — you are underperforming given your market position; structural or operational issues need diagnosis
- RMS is low and trending downward — you are losing ground to a competitor who will enjoy compounding cost advantages

**Ambiguous result — probe further:**
- If your ROA seems high but RMS is low, ask: are you an overperformer under a price umbrella? How sustainable is this?
- If RMS is high but ROA is low (below-band leader), ask: is this an investment phase or a structural problem?
- If the normative band is hard to plot, ask: are you defining your competitive set correctly?

## Agent Prompting Guidance

**Opening question:**
> "We're moving into Law 2 now — this is about understanding your market position, not just your costs. Let's start with the ROA/RMS chart. Do you have a sense of where you sit relative to competitors on both profitability and market share — and whether you're earning what your position should entitle you to?"

**Follow-up probes if stuck:**
- "Who are your direct competitors — the ones with the same types of customers, costs, and capabilities as you?"
- "Are you the market leader, a strong #2, or further back in the pack?"
- "Is your profitability roughly in line with your market position, or do you suspect you're over- or underperforming relative to peers?"
- "Have you ever tried to plot your ROA against your relative market share? Even rough estimates are useful here."

**Synthesising the finding:**
> "Based on what you've described, you appear to be a [position from Table 3.2] — [characteristics]. That means your strategic imperative is to [action]. Does that feel like an accurate read of where you stand?"

**Signal to move on:**
User has identified their position on the ROA/RMS spectrum, understands whether they are over- or underperforming relative to their market share, and has a working view of the strategic posture that implies.
