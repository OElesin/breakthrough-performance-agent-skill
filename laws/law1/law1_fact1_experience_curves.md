# Fact 1: Experience Curves
**Law:** Law 1 — Costs and prices always decline
**Position in Law:** Fact 1 of 3

---

## Purpose
Establish how your cost trajectory compares to competitors and to industry price movement, revealing whether you are gaining, losing, or holding competitive cost position as experience accumulates.

---

## Goal
Construct three experience curves:
1. An industry-wide price experience curve
2. Your own overall cost experience curve
3. Cost experience curves for chief competitors (where data permits)

Once your overall cost curve is in hand, construct experience curves for every major cost element — including labour, overhead, and outsourced materials and services.

---

## Diagnostic Question
> "How does your cost slope compare with those of your competitors? What is the slope of price changes in your industry right now, and how does your cost curve compare?"

---

## Data Required

- **Unit of experience definition**
  - What it is: The unit by which accumulated production is measured — ideally the unit of customer value, not just output units
  - Where to get it: Internal product/commercial team; align with how customers perceive and buy value

- **Cumulative annual unit production volume over time**
  - What it is: Total units produced each year, going as far back as possible
  - Where to get it: Internal operations/finance records; industry associations; market research reports
  - If unavailable: Estimate prior years using the formula below

- **Constant currency unit industry prices over time**
  - What it is: Industry average price per unit, adjusted for inflation to remove currency noise
  - Where to get it: Industry publications, pricing databases, third-party research

- **Constant currency unit costs — your company and competitors — over time**
  - What it is: Per-unit cost in real terms for your business and key rivals
  - Where to get it: Internal cost accounting; competitor financials (public filings, analyst reports); industry benchmarks
  - If unavailable: Use proxy indicators or management estimates with explicit assumptions

---

## Analytical Approach

### Step 1: Estimate accumulated experience if early data is missing
Use the following formula to estimate total experience prior to your first data year:

```
Total experience prior to year 1 = (2/3) × (volume in year 1 / CAGR)

Where CAGR = compound annual growth rate of volume from year 1 to the latest year
```

**Example:** If 100 units were produced in year 1 (2000) and 240 in year 5 (2005):
- CAGR = 19.1%
- Total experience prior to 2000 = (2/3) × (100 / 0.191) ≈ 350 units

Reality-check this estimate against expert knowledge, S-curve analysis, and historical growth rates. Adjust if the assumption of constant growth rate does not hold.

### Step 2: Plot the experience curves
- X-axis: Accumulated experience (log scale)
- Y-axis: Real price/cost per unit (log scale)
- Plot three lines: industry price, your cost, competitor cost(s)

### Step 3: Calculate the slope (experience curve percentage)
- A 90% curve means costs fall 10% each time cumulative volume doubles
- A 70% curve means costs fall 30% each time cumulative volume doubles
- Steeper slope = faster cost reduction with experience

### Step 4: Compare slopes
- Is your cost curve steeper (better) or shallower (worse) than competitors?
- Is the industry price curve steeper or shallower than your cost curve?
- What is the gap between your cost curve and the price curve — is it widening or narrowing?

---

## Interpretation Guide

**Healthy signal:**
- Your cost slope is steeper than competitors — you are extracting more learning per unit of experience
- Your cost curve is below the industry price curve with a widening gap — margin is expanding

**Warning signal:**
- Your cost slope is shallower than a competitor — they will eventually undercut you even at similar current volumes
- Price curve is falling faster than your cost curve — margin is being compressed by industry dynamics

**Ambiguous result — probe further:**
- If curves are parallel, ask: what would change the slope? Are there process innovations or scale plays available?
- If data is incomplete, make assumptions explicit and test sensitivity

## Additional Tools
The tools used to manage toward the experience curve span all major cost categories:

- **Supply chain:** supply-chain strategy, demand planning, inventory and order management, distribution management
- **Manufacturing:** manufacturing strategy, manufacturing footprint, plant improvement, Lean Six Sigma (LSS), outsourcing
- **Procurement:** supplier strategy, purchasing-cost reduction, vendor-performance management, supplier negotiation, organisation and process improvement
- **SG&A:** overhead optimisation (finance, HR, IT, legal); salesforce productivity
- **CAPEX management:** CAPEX management and budgeting, working-capital management, real estate optimisation

Refer to Appendix 2 of The Breakthrough Imperative for the full methodology on constructing experience curves.

## Agent Prompting Guidance

**Opening question:**
> "Let's start with experience curves — the foundation of your cost position analysis. Do you have a sense of how your unit costs have moved over time relative to your competitors, or is this something we need to construct from scratch?"

**Follow-up probes if stuck:**
- "What unit best captures the value your customers actually buy from you?"
- "How far back does your cost data go? Even rough historical data is useful."
- "Do you have any competitor cost data — even from public filings or analyst reports?"
- "What has been happening to industry prices over the last 3–5 years in real terms?"

**Synthesising the finding:**
Once the user has shared data or estimates, reflect back:
> "So your cost curve appears to be [steeper/shallower] than [competitor], and the industry price curve is [falling faster/slower] than your costs. That means your cost position is [improving/deteriorating]. Does that match your intuition?"

**Signal to move on:**
User has confirmed or estimated their relative cost slope and has a working view of whether their cost position is strengthening or weakening versus competitors and versus price.
