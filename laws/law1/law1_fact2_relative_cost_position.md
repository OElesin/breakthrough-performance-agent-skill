# Fact 2: Relative Cost Position Analysis
**Law:** Law 1 — Costs and prices always decline
**Position in Law:** Fact 2 of 3

---

## Purpose
Determine your practical full-potential cost position by comparing your unit costs element by element against competitors and best demonstrated practices — revealing exactly where you are losing or winning on cost and how much improvement is realistically achievable.

---

## Goal
Determine a company's practical full-potential cost position based on a comparison of unit costs with those of its competitors. Adjust the comparison for meaningful differences in your business model, product mix, or operational structure.

---

## Diagnostic Question
> "What are your costs compared with competitors? Who is most efficient and effective in priority areas? Where can you improve most relative to others?"

---

## Data Required

- **Product or service process flow**
  - What it is: An outline of the end-to-end value chain for your business and major competitors
  - Where to get it: Internal operations; industry maps; analyst reports

- **Total cost per unit for your business by major cost components**
  - What it is: Unit economics broken down by cost category (labour, materials, overhead, etc.)
  - Where to get it: Internal cost accounting / management accounts

- **Total cost per unit for competitors**
  - What it is: Equivalent cost breakdown for key rivals
  - Where to get it: Public filings, analyst reports, industry benchmarks, triangulated estimates

- **Internal and external best demonstrated practices by activity or major cost element**
  - What it is: The lowest observed cost for each step in the value chain, from any company in or adjacent to your industry
  - Where to get it: Benchmarking studies, industry publications, operational due diligence, expert interviews

---

## Analytical Approach

### Two complementary methods — use both where possible

**Top-down approach**
Compare competitors' overall cost structure with your own at the macro level. Use publicly available financial data to establish the overall cost gap before drilling into components.

**Bottom-up approach**
Gather primary data on each major cost element for yourself and key competitors. Triangulate using data from multiple sources to reinforce and confirm hypotheses. Define key process and business-practice differences for major cost elements, assess the impact of these differences, then test conclusions against overall financial data.

### Five-step RCP process (Figure A3.4)

| Step | Description | Key Success Factor |
|------|-------------|-------------------|
| 1. Map the business value chain | End-to-end from raw materials to finished product or delivery | Map from customer's perspective, not internal org chart |
| 2. Identify cost elements and drivers | What drives cost at each step | Tie costs to operational activities, not accounting categories |
| 3. Build, compare, and reality-check cost bars | Stack cost bars category by category for you vs. competitors | Reality-check results against similar companies |
| 4. Calculate practical full-potential cost position and savings | Focus on areas with greatest cost-saving potential | Adjust for differences vis-à-vis competitors (e.g. product mix, plant locations, vertical integration) |
| 5. Prioritise action initiatives | Determine what to act on first | Prioritise based on value and difficulty/cost of achieving |

### Building the aspirational benchmark
Best demonstrated practices analysis works by taking the lowest-cost provider's costs at each step of the value chain and combining them into a hypothetical "best of the best" cost position. This total will be lower than any single company in the industry — it represents an aspirational target. This is intentional: your competitors will be improving while you improve, so your targets must be more aggressive than where your lowest-cost competitor is today. You can then determine how far you can realistically close the gap against that aspirational benchmark.

## Interpretation Guide

**Strategic insights RCP unlocks:**
- Where competitors have their biggest cost advantage
- What is driving their profitability
- How much flexibility they might have in a competitive price battle

**Tactical insights RCP unlocks:**
- Where to focus cost-reduction efforts (labour? raw materials? overhead?)
- Which cost elements might decrease significantly with scale and experience
- Which cost elements might benefit from different business practices

**Healthy signal:**
- Your cost bars are at or below competitor levels in the highest-value activities
- Your full-potential cost position is achievable within a credible improvement plan

**Warning signal:**
- A competitor has a structural cost advantage in a core activity that cannot be matched without a business model change
- Your aspirational benchmark is so far from your current position that the gap is not closeable within the planning horizon

**Ambiguous result — probe further:**
- If cost data is incomplete, ask: which cost elements matter most strategically? Focus triangulation there first
- If the gap seems surprisingly small or large, reality-check against overall margin differences

## Additional Tools
Benchmarking against best demonstrated practices reveals the performance gap. Implementation tools span all cost management domains:

- **Supply chain:** supply-chain strategy, demand planning, inventory and order management, distribution management
- **Manufacturing:** manufacturing strategy, manufacturing footprint, plant improvement, Lean Six Sigma (LSS), outsourcing
- **Procurement:** supplier strategy, purchasing-cost reduction, vendor-performance management, supplier negotiation, organisation and process improvement
- **SG&A:** overhead optimisation (finance, HR, IT, legal); salesforce productivity
- **CAPEX management:** CAPEX management and budgeting, working-capital management, real estate optimisation

## Agent Prompting Guidance

**Opening question:**
> "Now let's look at your relative cost position — not just how your costs move over time, but how they stack up against competitors right now, element by element. Do you have a sense of where you are more or less expensive than your key rivals?"

**Follow-up probes if stuck:**
- "Walk me through your value chain — what are the major steps from input to delivery to the customer?"
- "Which cost categories are the biggest as a share of your total unit cost?"
- "Where do you suspect a competitor has a structural cost advantage over you?"
- "Have you ever done any benchmarking against best-in-class operators, inside or outside your industry?"

**Synthesising the finding:**
> "So your biggest cost gaps versus competitors appear to be in [areas]. The most achievable improvement opportunity looks like [element], and the hardest structural gap to close is [element]. Does that feel right?"

**Signal to move on:**
User has a working view of their relative cost position by major cost element, has identified where the largest gaps are, and has a rough sense of what is achievable versus structural.
